What MVHR does in a home

MVHR is a whole-home ventilation system. It supplies fresh, filtered air to living rooms and bedrooms, and it extracts stale, humid air from kitchens, bathrooms and utility rooms. As the two air streams pass through a heat exchanger, warmth from the outgoing air can be transferred to the incoming air, with heat recovery commonly described in the range of 70 to 90 percent depending on the unit, the installation and the operating conditions. That process is particularly relevant in well-insulated and airtight homes, where uncontrolled draughts are limited and ventilation needs to be planned rather than left to gaps in the building fabric. A summer bypass, where fitted, allows incoming air to bypass the heat exchanger in warmer conditions, so that heat is not added when it is not wanted. The result is a system that provides continuous background ventilation, rather than relying only on opening windows or on intermittent extractor fans.

Design, installation and commissioning

Drumlin Air approaches MVHR as a designed system. Design-stage ductwork planning looks at floor plans, room uses, ceiling voids, unit location and duct routes before installation begins, so that supply and extract points, duct sizes and terminal positions are considered together. That early planning helps with airflow, noise, pressure and access for maintenance, and it helps coordinate with other trades on a new build or a major renovation. Installation then follows the design, with the unit, ductwork, valves, grilles and controls fitted as a complete system. After installation, room-by-room commissioning checks the airflow at each supply and extract point against the design intention, so that living spaces receive fresh air and wet rooms are properly extracted. That commissioning step gives the homeowner a clear record of how the system was set up, and it is an important part of turning a design into a working installation.

Servicing and everyday use

Servicing is part of living with an MVHR system. Filters need attention in line with the manufacturer guidance for the unit, so that incoming air remains filtered and airflow is not restricted. The unit and ductwork should remain accessible for inspection and servicing, and occupants should understand the controls, including normal and boost settings for cooking and showering. Drumlin Air provides servicing as part of its MVHR work, and a clear explanation of filter care and control use helps homeowners keep the system working as designed. If a system seems noisy, stuffy or difficult to use, a check of settings, valves and filters is a sensible first step, rather than switching the system off, because an airtight home relies on its planned ventilation for a steady supply of fresh air.

New builds, renovations and existing homes

MVHR can be considered for a new build, where ductwork can be integrated from the start, for a major renovation, where ceilings and service routes are open, and for an existing home, where retrofitting requires a careful survey of available voids, routes and plant locations. In each case, the home, its construction, its airtightness and its heating system matter, and so do the relevant building considerations, including ventilation under Part F and energy conservation under Part L, and the move toward Nearly Zero Energy Buildings, known as NZEB.
